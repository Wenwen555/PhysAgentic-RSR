EXPERIMENTAL REASONING
A reaction at a single control setting often cannot identify a quantity. Choose
several distinct, deliberately controlled settings that constrain your selected
parameter; compare responses under matching nuisance conditions. Derive how the
setting changes the response, choose the next setting adaptively, and use the
contrasting measurements to produce a numerical estimate and an honest interval
or bracket. Repeating a safe-looking action without estimating anything is not
an identification experiment. A one-sided qualitative signal is not a calibrated
point estimate. Bound extrapolation and explicitly retain unobservable dimensions.

Prefer an interpretable governing physical quantity over an empirical optimal
control setting when the available channels can distinguish the quantity from
contact/control effects. Before stopping at an effective response proxy, evaluate
whether staged experiments and independent sensing can remove those confounders.
Create the needed measurement conditions (calibration, support state, repeatable
configuration, settling) instead of rejecting a physical measurement model solely
because those conditions were absent in the original task execution.
Once a usable parameter region exists, choose validation settings inside or near
that region with predicted load/contact margin. Do not jump to untested geometric
extremes just to avoid repeating a setting. Preserve every valid estimate and its
source measurements even if a later action or recovery fails. Experiment control
status and accumulated physical evidence are separate outputs.

Do not require identification of every unknown limit before ending parameter
acquisition. An observed sustained load gives a setting-specific capacity lower
bound, not a maximum capacity. Parameter estimation can be sufficient for selecting
a conservative candidate while contact capacity and full-task success remain to
be tested separately. Do not keep seeking arbitrary contrasts after compatible
estimates and relevant local validation have stopped changing the task decision.

Write the measurement equations, units, frames, assumptions and failure tests.
Parameter precision is not an end in itself: stop when the resulting action has
adequate margin relative to remaining uncertainty, or report non-identifiability.
Do not force a numerical conclusion when confounded observations disagree.
Intervals derived from resolution/brackets are not statistical confidence levels.
